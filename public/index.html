SubjectIssue No. 1: Hannah Clarke, the developer who reads before she rewrites

Issue No. 1

Hannah Clarke

Full-stack developer, Leeds, UK

Hannah started out building websites for her dad's furniture shop and never really stopped. After eight years in product teams she went freelance, mostly so she could work closer to the people using what she builds.

She likes small, careful changes over big rewrites, and she writes things down as she goes. Lately she's been curious about how far small teams can get with AI tools, and where a developer still makes the difference.

What she does best

Taking an app that works on a good day and making it work every day. Most of her clients come to her with a Laravel or Supabase backend that grew faster than anyone planned.

Selected projects

Studio booking system
Her longest-running client is a family-run group of yoga studios. What began as a simple class timetable has grown, one request at a time, into the system they use for bookings, memberships, payments and waiting lists. Teachers check their classes on their phones, and the owners finally have one place to see how each studio is doing.
Laravel, Livewire, Stripe

Supplier portal
A small food wholesaler was spending most mornings on the phone, chasing suppliers about deliveries. Hannah built a portal where suppliers see what's been ordered, confirm what they can send and flag anything that's running late. It's a modest app, but the team says it gave them back an hour a day.
Laravel, Postgres

Fitness app launch
A founder had built a workout-tracking app with Lovable and was two weeks from launch. The app looked finished, but any logged-in user could read everyone else's data, and sign-ups failed on some phones. Hannah spent a week on database security and the sign-up flow, then stayed on for the first month after launch.
Supabase, React

Open for work

Takes on: fixes, small builds and ongoing work
Can start: mid-November, two days a week
Works with: Laravel, Supabase, React

Second cup

Before you change anything, write down how the app should behave. Half the bugs I fix come from nobody agreeing on that.
  • Tool: TablePlus, for looking at real data before guessing
  • Website: Laracasts, still where she goes to learn something new
  • Book: The Pragmatic Programmer by Andrew Hunt and David Thomas
  • Quote: "Make it work, make it right, make it fast." Kent Beck

A few honest questions

Is it actually free?
Yes, totally. It's our way of giving back to the developer community.
How are developers chosen?
By hand. We look at real portfolios, GitHub activity and work history. No algorithm, and nobody can buy their way in.
Do featured developers pay to be included?
No. Being featured is never paid for. We can't promise anyone will hire you, but it puts you and your work in front of people who might.
